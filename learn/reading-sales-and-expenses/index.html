Reading a small business's sales and expenses, practised with play money

Short answer: To read a small business's numbers, start with three things: sales (money coming in), expenses (money going out) and the difference between them (profit or loss) over the same period. Then ask whether those numbers are steady, growing or shrinking, and whether they can be checked.

What is the difference between sales and profit?

Sales are everything customers paid. Profit is what is left after expenses such as stock, rent, salaries, fuel and data are paid. A shop can have big sales and still make a loss.

Which expenses are easy to miss?

Owner's own pay, generator fuel, equipment repairs, loan repayments, transport, and money set aside for slow months are commonly left out of quick estimates.

What questions should I ask about the numbers?

Over what period? Are they from records, bank statements or estimates? Are some months seasonal? Who prepared them, and can they be independently checked?

How can I practise this with play money?

In Kobo Share you use practice credits on fictional example businesses and watch simulated daily practice scores rise and fall. The scores are random-style simulations for learning, not forecasts or real results.
