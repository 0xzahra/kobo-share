How SEC Nigeria's crowdfunding rules work

Short answer: In Nigeria, investment crowdfunding is regulated by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Nigeria). Businesses may only raise money from the public through a crowdfunding portal (intermediary) registered with the SEC, within fundraising limits the SEC sets; SEC Nigeria's own guidance mentions annual fundraising of up to ₦100 million. Unregistered platforms are illegal.

Who is allowed to run a crowdfunding platform?

Only companies registered with SEC Nigeria as crowdfunding intermediaries/portals. The registration requirements, including fees and portal information, are published at SEC Nigeria: Crowdfunding Intermediary Registration Requirements.

How much can a business raise?

SEC Nigeria's guidance (SEC Nigeria: Insights on Crowdfunding Policies) says the rules allow up to ₦100 million in annual fundraising. Limits can depend on the size category of the business and can change, so always read the current SEC material.

Which platforms are licensed?

As context only: when checked in October 2026, SEC Nigeria's SEC Nigeria: Registered FinTech Operators page listed Obelix 4.1.1 Alternative Ltd, PropCrowdy Ltd and Pennytree Business Ltd under Crowdfunding, and further crowdfunding platforms appear in SEC Nigeria: Find a Registered Operator. This is not a recommendation. Status can change, so always verify on the SEC site yourself.

Is Kobo Share a crowdfunding platform?

No. Kobo Share raises no money, lists no real businesses and is not registered with SEC Nigeria. It is a practice simulator for learning.
